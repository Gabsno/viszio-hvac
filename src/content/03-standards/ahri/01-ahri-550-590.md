AHRI Standard 550/590 is the reason a designer can compare two chillers from two different manufacturers and trust the numbers. It defines how water-chilling packages are rated, and this guide explains the rating basis, the part-load metric and the certification program built on it.

## What the standard is and who publishes it

The standard is published by the Air-Conditioning, Heating, and Refrigeration Institute, known as AHRI, the North American trade association whose central role is product performance certification. The full title of the inch-pound edition is Performance Rating of Water-chilling and Heat Pump Water-heating Packages Using the Vapor Compression Cycle. It has a metric sibling, Standard 551/591, which carries the same content in SI units. Together they govern factory-made vapour-compression water-chilling and water-heating packages, whether the condenser is water-cooled, air-cooled or evaporatively cooled.

The standard's stated purpose is to establish definitions, test requirements, rating requirements, minimum data requirements for published ratings, and the conversions and conformance conditions that go with them. It is explicitly written for the guidance of the whole industry, including manufacturers, engineers, installers and regulators.

## Why the standard exists

A chiller's capacity and efficiency depend heavily on the conditions it is tested at: water temperatures, water flow rates and fouling allowance all move the numbers. Without a common rating basis, every manufacturer could quote performance at whatever conditions flattered its product, and an engineer would have no honest way to compare. AHRI 550/590 exists to fix the conditions, the test method and the way results are published, so that a rated capacity and efficiency mean the same thing on every manufacturer's data sheet.

## How the standard is organised

The standard follows a clear sequence. Early sections set the purpose and scope and then define terms, including the capacity and efficiency quantities. A test requirements section ties ratings to a defined method of test. A rating requirements section sets the standard rating conditions and the calculations. A minimum data requirements section specifies exactly what a manufacturer must publish. Further sections cover conversions, symbols and conformance, and a set of normative and informative appendices supplies the test method, the derivation of the part-load metric and various correction procedures.

## The core engineering concept

The standard rests on a defined rating basis and a part-load metric.

### Standard rating conditions

Performance is rated at specified standard rating conditions: defined entering and leaving water temperatures, defined flow or temperature difference, and a defined fouling allowance for the heat exchangers. Net refrigerating capacity is the cooling available to the external load, and efficiency is reported either as a coefficient of performance or as power input per unit of capacity. Because every chiller is rated at the same conditions, a designer comparing data sheets is comparing like with like.

### Full load efficiency and the IPLV

Full-load efficiency describes the chiller at its rated capacity, but a chiller rarely runs fully loaded. Cooling demand rises and falls, so most of a chiller's operating hours are spent at part load. To capture this, the standard defines the Integrated Part Load Value, or IPLV. The IPLV combines efficiency measured at several load points into a single weighted number. The load points are weighted to reflect how much of a typical year's operation occurs near each load, so lighter loads, where a chiller spends much of its time, carry significant weight. A related figure, the Non-standard Part Load Value or NPLV, is used when a chiller is rated at conditions other than the standard ones.

The practical lesson is that two chillers with identical full-load efficiency can have quite different IPLVs, and for most buildings the IPLV is the better predictor of annual energy use.

### Why a common basis enables fair comparison

Because the standard fixes the test conditions, the test method and the published data, an engineer can lay two manufacturers' selections side by side and trust that a difference in rated capacity or efficiency is a real difference in the machines, not an artefact of generous test assumptions. This is the single most important service the standard provides.

## Tolerances and the certification program

No physical test reproduces a rated value exactly, so the standard defines tolerances: allowable bands within which a tested unit's measured capacity and efficiency may fall and still be considered to confirm the published rating. Separately, AHRI operates a certification program in which independent, randomly selected production units are tested against their published ratings. A chiller line that carries the AHRI certification mark has had its ratings verified by a third party, which gives the designer and owner confidence beyond the manufacturer's own claims.

## How an engineer uses it day to day

When selecting a chiller, an engineer reads the manufacturer's rated net capacity and efficiency knowing they sit on the 550/590 basis, checks the IPLV against the building's expected load profile rather than relying on full-load efficiency alone, confirms the unit carries AHRI certification where that assurance is wanted, and applies the standard's correction procedures if the project's actual conditions differ from the standard rating conditions.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls

A common mistake is comparing chillers on full-load efficiency only, ignoring the IPLV that better reflects real operation. Another is comparing a unit rated at standard conditions against one rated at non-standard conditions without recognising the NPLV distinction. Designers also forget that ratings assume a defined fouling allowance and flow, so field conditions that differ will shift real performance. Finally, assuming every published rating is third-party verified is wrong; only units in the certification program carry that assurance.
