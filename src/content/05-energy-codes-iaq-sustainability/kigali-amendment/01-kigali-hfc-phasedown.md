The Kigali Amendment is the regulation reshaping which refrigerants HVAC engineers can specify. Understanding it is no longer optional, because it sets a binding timetable that determines what will be in the cylinder on the next job.

## From ozone to climate

The story starts with the Montreal Protocol of 1987, one of the most successful environmental treaties ever signed. It addressed the hole in the ozone layer by phasing out ozone-depleting substances. Chlorofluorocarbons, the CFCs, went first. Hydrochlorofluorocarbons, the HCFCs, including the once-common R-22, are being phased out behind them.

The chemical industry's answer was the hydrofluorocarbons, the HFCs, such as R-410A and R-134a. HFCs contain no chlorine, so they do not damage ozone. The problem is that they are extremely powerful greenhouse gases. The Montreal Protocol fixed the ozone problem and inadvertently created a climate problem.

## What the Kigali Amendment does

The Kigali Amendment, agreed in 2016, adds HFCs to the Montreal Protocol. It does not phase them out completely. It phases them down, reducing their production and consumption in steps over decades toward a low residual level. The aim is to avoid a large amount of future warming by curbing HFC growth before it locks in.

### Global warming potential explained

Global warming potential, or GWP, measures how much heat a gas traps relative to carbon dioxide over a chosen period, usually 100 years. Carbon dioxide has a GWP of 1 by definition. Many common HFCs have a GWP in the hundreds or thousands.

| Refrigerant | Type | Approximate GWP |
|---|---|---|
| R-410A | HFC blend | Around 2088 |
| R-32 | HFC | Around 675 |
| R-454B | A2L blend | Around 466 |
| R-290 propane | Hydrocarbon | Around 3 |
| R-744 carbon dioxide | Natural | 1 |

Releasing one kilogram of R-410A has roughly the same warming effect as releasing two tonnes of carbon dioxide, which is why leak-tightness matters so much.

## The phase down schedule

Kigali splits countries into groups with different start dates, recognising that developing economies need more time.

- **Developed countries** began their phase-down first, in 2019.
- **Group 1 developing countries**, the larger group including China and much of Latin America, freeze consumption later and then step down.
- **Group 2 developing countries**, which includes most of Africa, India and several Gulf states, have the latest schedule, with a freeze followed by a staged reduction toward an 80 to 85 percent cut by the late 2040s.

The later African timetable does not mean engineers can ignore it. Equipment is global, manufacturers shift their product lines to the new refrigerants worldwide, and high-GWP gas becomes scarce and expensive long before any legal deadline.

## Lower GWP alternatives and their trade offs

No single refrigerant replaces HFCs everywhere. Each option carries a trade-off.

- **R-32** has a much lower GWP than R-410A and good efficiency, but it is mildly flammable, classed A2L.
- **R-454B** is an A2L blend with lower GWP again, replacing R-410A in many new split and packaged systems.
- **R-290 propane** has a near-zero GWP and excellent efficiency, but it is highly flammable, class A3, which limits charge size and applications.
- **R-744 carbon dioxide** is non-flammable and non-toxic with a GWP of 1, but it runs at very high pressures and needs specialised equipment.
- **Ammonia** is an outstanding industrial refrigerant with zero GWP, but it is toxic and flammable.

The A2L mild-flammability class is the practical reality for most commercial HVAC. A2L refrigerants will burn, but with difficulty, and updated safety standards govern charge limits, ventilation, leak detection and room sizing.

## What HVAC engineers must do

Design for the transition. Select equipment that uses lower-GWP refrigerants so an installation is not obsolete within its service life. Build for leak-tightness, because a refrigerant with a GWP of 466 still causes real harm if it leaks. Learn the A2L safety standards and apply the correct charge limits, leak detection and ventilation. Train installation and service technicians on safe handling of mildly flammable refrigerants.

> 🇬🇭 **Ghana context**
> Ghana has ratified the Kigali Amendment and sits in the Group 2 developing-country schedule, with the Ghana Environmental Protection Agency administering refrigerant controls. Because almost all refrigerant and equipment is imported, the global shift to lower-GWP gases will reach the Ghanaian market through supply availability and price well before any domestic deadline. Engineers specifying systems in Accra should choose lower-GWP equipment now and ensure technicians are trained for the safe handling of A2L refrigerants such as R-32 and R-454B.
