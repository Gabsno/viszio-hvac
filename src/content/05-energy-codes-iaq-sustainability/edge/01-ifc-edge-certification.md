EDGE is a green-building standard and software tool built specifically for emerging markets. For an HVAC engineer working anywhere in Africa, it is one of the most practical sustainability frameworks to understand, because the energy target at its core is largely won or lost on the mechanical systems.

## What EDGE is

EDGE stands for Excellence in Design for Greater Efficiencies. It was created by IFC, the International Finance Corporation, which is part of the World Bank Group. It exists because older green-building rating systems, while rigorous, were often too slow, too expensive and too tailored to wealthy markets to gain traction in developing economies. EDGE was designed from the start to be fast, affordable and data-driven.

EDGE is two things at once. It is a certification a building can earn, and it is a free software application that any designer can use to model a project and see, in minutes, whether it meets the standard.

## The core requirement

To be EDGE certified, a building must demonstrate a reduction of at least 20 percent in each of three categories, measured against a base-case building representing standard local practice.

| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Energy | At least 20 percent less than base case |
| Water | At least 20 percent less than base case |
| Embodied energy in materials | At least 20 percent less than base case |

The base case is important. EDGE does not compare a Ghanaian building to a building in Europe. It builds a base case from local climate data, local construction practice and local utility costs, then asks the design to beat it by a fifth in each category. This makes the target meaningful and locally fair.

## The EDGE app and certification levels

The EDGE app is free to use. A designer enters the building type, location, floor areas, envelope, lighting and HVAC choices, and the software calculates the percentage savings and the financial payback of each measure. It turns sustainability from a vague aspiration into numbers a developer can act on before construction.

There are three certification levels.

- **EDGE Certified.** The entry level, achieved at 20 percent savings in all three categories.
- **EDGE Advanced.** Requires at least 40 percent on-site energy savings, a markedly more efficient building.
- **EDGE Zero Carbon.** Requires 100 percent energy savings or offsets, so the building's operational carbon is neutralised.

Certification involves an independent audit at design stage and again after construction, so the claimed savings are verified rather than just modelled.

## Why EDGE suits African markets

EDGE fits African real estate for concrete reasons. It is low cost compared with other certifications, so it does not price out mid-sized developers. It is fast, because the free app gives an immediate read on compliance. It is locally calibrated, so the base case reflects real conditions in Accra, Lagos or Nairobi rather than an imported assumption. And because IFC is a development finance institution, EDGE certification can unlock access to green finance and concessional lending, giving developers a commercial reason to certify.

## HVAC at the centre of the energy target

In a hot climate, cooling dominates the energy bill, so the 20 percent energy reduction is largely an HVAC problem to solve. The levers an engineer pulls are familiar: high-efficiency chillers or VRF systems, correct equipment sizing rather than habitual oversizing, variable-speed fans and pumps, economizers where the climate allows, good controls and effective commissioning. Envelope measures such as glazing and shading reduce the cooling load, but it is the mechanical design that converts a reduced load into a reduced bill. An HVAC engineer who understands EDGE can steer a project to certification while the design is still flexible.

> 🇬🇭 **Ghana context**
> EDGE has gained real traction in Ghana, with certified residential, commercial and hospitality projects across Accra and beyond. In Accra's hot-humid climate the energy savings are dominated by cooling, so HVAC selection and sizing decide whether a project clears the 20 percent threshold. The Ghana Energy Commission's building energy efficiency work and EDGE certification reinforce each other, and EDGE certification can also help Ghanaian developers access green finance lines offered through IFC and partner banks.
